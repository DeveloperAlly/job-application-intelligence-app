# 03 Evals: the hiring standard a tailored resume and cover letter is judged against

Purpose: convert "decades of research into what lands well" into a written, cited, checkable rubric,
so that every downstream quality requirement points at something falsifiable.

Scope: senior technical and developer relations hiring (engineering, AI and agent systems, developer
relations), including remote and international applications. Where the evidence for senior roles
differs from general advice, the senior finding is preferred and the divergence is stated on the row
or in Thresholds. No personal data, career history or applicant identity was sent outward to produce
this file; only the general standard was researched.

Every row below carries a URL that was actually fetched in producing this file. A source that was not
fetched is not cited. A number without a fetched source or a runnable command is not present.

Coverage of the six things the owner said would make her refuse to send:

1. poor styling: D-01, D-09
2. poor word choices: D-02
3. content not matching the job description: D-03
4. the wrong portfolio pieces selected: D-04
5. a cover letter of sloppy AI prose rather than high level language: D-05
6. nothing matching the research into what lands well: D-06

D-07, D-08 and D-09 are additional dimensions the fetched research supports.

## Thresholds

Environment note, established by command on this machine: `which pdftotext` returns "pdftotext not found"
and `python3 -c "import pdfplumber"` returns ModuleNotFoundError. `python3 -c "import pypdf"` succeeds.
All shell checks above are therefore written against python3 and pypdf, which are present, not against
pdftotext or pdfplumber, which are absent.

- D-01 pages: fail if more than 2. Basis: the eye tracking study's own guidance states a two page maximum, and the 482 reviewer simulation found reviewers 2.9 times as likely to prefer two page resumes for managerial roles, 2.6 times for mid level, 1.4 times for entry level. For senior technical applications the general "one page" advice is contradicted by this evidence and is not enforced.
- D-01 columns: fail if more than 1. Basis: the eye tracking study lists multiple columns among the characteristics of the worst performing resumes, and the ATS vendor states that tables and columns "will put words, and sadly sometimes letters, on different lines".
- D-01 font families: `threshold unestablished because no fetched source specifies a maximum number of font families; the fetched evidence specifies only "a clear font"`.
- D-02 responsibility phrase openers: fail if 1 or more. The count of zero is definitional for a property that must never occur, not a measured optimum. What the research establishes is direction and magnitude, not a count: a field experiment on roughly half a million jobseekers found that algorithmic writing assistance produced an 8 percent increase in the probability of being hired, and the eye tracking study's guidance is short declarative sentences with information carrying words first.
- D-02 spelling errors: fail if 1 or more. Same definitional basis as above.
- D-03 required term misses: fail if 1 or more. Basis: the ATS vendor documents full text keyword search over resume text, and the fetched documentation does not disclose synonym expansion or fuzzy matching, so a missing exact string cannot be assumed forgiven.
- D-03 key skill overlap: fail below 50 percent. Basis: the hiring platform's skills based talent pool is defined as candidates whose recent roles have "at least 50 percent overlap in key skills with the target job". Caveat: that is the platform's analytical definition of a talent pool, not a stated employer decision rule, so 50 percent is a floor borrowed from a matching model rather than a measured callback boundary.
- D-04 links returning other than HTTP 200: fail if 1 or more. Definitional.
- D-04 forks presented as original work: fail if 1 or more. Basis: the CSCW study found that whether a project was original or forked is trivially verifiable from the profile icon, so the misrepresentation is caught at effectively zero cost by the reader.
- D-04 recency window: `threshold unestablished because the CSCW study reports that employers check for evidence of recent activity but names no time window`.
- D-04 maximum number of links: `threshold unestablished because the CSCW study establishes that evaluation cost governs which traces get inspected, and that contributions to a high status project are reliable but hard to verify, without naming a number of links at which a reader stops`.
- D-05 tailored versus generic: binary, fail if the letter is not specific to this posting. Basis: the 7,287 application field experiment found tailored cover letters produced a 53 percent higher callback rate than no cover letter, and 81 percent of surveyed professionals valued tailored letters significantly over generic ones.
- D-05 cross letter sentence similarity ceiling: `threshold unestablished because no fetched source gives a similarity number at which a reader perceives a letter as recycled`.
- D-05 count of company specific verifiable details: `threshold unestablished because no fetched source counts them`. What is established is that after an AI cover letter tool was introduced on a large labour platform, the correlation between a letter's textual alignment with the job post and callbacks fell by 51 percent, and employers shifted toward prior work histories as the signal. Alignment alone therefore no longer carries the letter; verifiable specifics and prior work history do.
- D-06 enforced rules with no row in this file: fail if 1 or more. Definitional, and it is the mechanism that keeps this file the authority rather than a decoration.
- D-07 parse failures: fail if 1 or more of the three standard section titles is missing from extracted text, or if any letter spacing artefact is found. Basis: the ATS vendor instructs the use of "typical names for section titles like 'Education', 'Work Experience', 'Personal Details'" and documents that unusual character spacing and font choice "can result in spaces being created or lost".
- D-07 file size: fail above the vendor upload limit of 100 MB stated in the fetched vendor documentation. The widely repeated claim that this vendor will not parse a file over 2.5 MB does not appear anywhere in the vendor documentation that was actually fetched, so that number is withdrawn and not used as a threshold.
- D-08 numeric claims with no source record: fail if 1 or more. Definitional. Supported in direction by the CSCW finding that employers treat traces they can verify as more reliable than resume assertions because they are difficult to fake, and by the platform study finding that employers shifted toward verifiable prior work history when written signal degraded.
- D-09 position of the target role title: `threshold unestablished because the fetched eye tracking evidence describes an F and E shaped scan and a top of page overview, but names no fraction of the page`. What is established: in the 232 user eyetracking study, "the first two paragraphs must state the most important information" and subheads, paragraphs and bullets should "start with information carrying words".

What today's behaviour would score: nothing is generated yet, so there is no rendered resume or cover
letter to run any of these checks against, and today's score is 0 of 9 dimensions measurable, with every
check running over an empty set, which is vacuous rather than passing.

## Sources

Fetched and used.

- https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html PRIMARY, with a stated weakness. First party release of the 2018 Ladders recruiter eye tracking study. Empirical eye tracking data collection, reporting a 7.4 second average initial screen (up from 6 seconds in 2012), and naming what the best and worst performing resumes did: simple layouts, clearly marked section and title headers, a clear font, F and E pattern flow, bold job titles with bulleted lists, versus cluttered layouts, long sentences, multiple columns, little white space, missing headers, keyword overuse. Weakness: the sample size and full methodology are not disclosed in any source that could be fetched, and the study was never peer reviewed; secondary reporting puts the sample at 30 recruiters. Rows resting on it are paired with a second source.
- https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/ PRIMARY. Nielsen Norman Group eyetracking study of 232 users, published 2006, and the origin of the F shaped scanning pattern finding. Credible because it is a controlled eyetracking study with a stated sample, from a research group whose method is published rather than sold. Caveat: it studies web page reading, not resumes specifically; the resume specific application of the same pattern comes from the eye tracking study above.
- https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/ OPINION. Trade press reporting of the Ladders study, not independent data. Used only as corroboration that the layout findings were reported consistently, since the underlying study PDF could not be fetched.
- https://www.resumego.net/research/one-or-two-page-resumes/ PRIMARY. Published simulation with disclosed methodology: 482 recruiters, hiring managers, HR professionals and C suite executives, 7,712 resumes, matched one page and two page pairs (350 to 500 words versus 700 to 850 words). Results: 1.4 times preference for two pages at entry level, 2.6 times at mid level, 2.9 times at managerial level; mean score 8.6 versus 7.1, 21 percent higher; 2 minutes 24 seconds spent on one page versus 4 minutes 5 seconds on two pages. Weakness: run by a commercial resume writing service, so the sponsor benefits from a finding that longer, professionally written resumes win.
- https://www.resumego.net/research/cover-letters/ PRIMARY. Field experiment: 7,287 fictitious applications submitted between 15 July 2019 and 10 January 2020 across ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor and Indeed, with three arms (no letter, generic letter, tailored letter). Reported result: tailored cover letters produced a 53 percent higher callback rate than no cover letter; 81 percent of surveyed professionals valued tailored letters significantly over generic ones. Note: the per arm callback percentages are published only inside chart images and could not be read from the fetched page, so they are not quoted anywhere in this file. Same sponsor weakness as above.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25054 PRIMARY. "Signaling in the Age of AI: Evidence from Cover Letters", Cui, Dias and Ye, September 2025 (revised November 2025). Difference in differences study of the introduction of an AI cover letter writing tool on a large online labour platform, with application level data on access and use. Findings used here: access raised textual alignment between letters and job posts and raised callbacks; time spent editing AI drafts is positively correlated with hiring success; the correlation between textual alignment and callbacks fell by 51 percent after introduction; employers shifted toward alternative signals including prior work history. Credible because it uses platform level administrative data and a stated identification strategy. Caveat: a preprint, not yet peer reviewed, and the setting is an online labour platform rather than a corporate hiring funnel.
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08083 PRIMARY. "Algorithmic Writing Assistance on Jobseekers' Resumes Increases Hires", van Inwegen, Munyikwa and Horton, January 2023. Randomised field experiment with nearly half a million jobseekers in an online labour market. Treated jobseekers experienced an 8 percent increase in the probability of being hired, with no evidence employers were less satisfied. This is the strongest causal evidence in this file that writing quality itself, not just content, changes hiring outcomes.
- https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~xia/resources/Documents/Marlow-cscw13.pdf PRIMARY. Marlow and Dabbish, "Activity Traces and Signals in Software Developer Recruitment and Hiring", CSCW 2013, peer reviewed, Carnegie Mellon. Method: semi structured interviews with 13 GitHub users (7 employers who used GitHub to evaluate candidates, 6 job seekers), recruited from a screening questionnaire sent to 200 GitHub members, of whom 128 responded. Findings used here: profile cues were treated as more reliable indicators of technical ability and motivation than resume information, because work actions are transparent and behaviour traces are hard to manipulate; contributions accepted to a high status project act as a community seal of approval and are reliable but hard to verify; active open source involvement is reliable and easy to verify; original versus forked is visible at a glance from an icon; evaluation cost governs which traces employers actually inspect. This is the senior technical specific evidence for portfolio selection. Caveat: 2013, and 7 employers is a small qualitative sample.
- https://resources.workable.com/stories-and-insights/how-ATS-reads-resumes PRIMARY. ATS vendor documentation of its own parser behaviour. States that headers and footers "often get interspersed with the main body of text", that tables and columns "will put words, and sadly sometimes letters, on different lines", that images and scanned PDFs cause character errors, that unusual character spacing and font choice can create or lose spaces, that section titles should use typical names, and that a PDF must be exported from a word processor rather than scanned.
- https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360052218132-Supported-formats-for-resumes-cover-letters-and-other-candidate-uploads PRIMARY. ATS vendor documentation. Accepted formats: .doc, .docx, .pdf, .rtf, .txt, up to 100 MB. Also states that externally linked images are not loaded when a resume is converted to PDF for preview. Note for the record: the fetched page does not contain the 2.5 MB parsing limit widely attributed to this vendor, so that figure is not used.
- https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/115004600186-Search-resumes-for-keywords PRIMARY. ATS vendor documentation confirming that recruiters run full text keyword search across resume text. The article does not disclose whether matching is exact or fuzzy, or whether synonyms are expanded; that silence is why D-03 requires exact strings.
- https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/skills-based-hiring-march-2025.pdf PRIMARY. Hiring platform data report, LinkedIn Economic Graph Research Institute, Skills-Based Hiring 2025 Report, 3 March 2025, using platform data through January 2025. Used for one thing only: its definition of a skills based talent pool as candidates whose roles in the last five years have at least 50 percent overlap in key skills with the target job.
